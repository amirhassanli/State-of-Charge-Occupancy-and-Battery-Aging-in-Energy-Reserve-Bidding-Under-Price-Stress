When Occupancy Aging Changes a CVaR Energy–Reserve Bid: A SMARD Diagnostic

Amir Hassanli and Ali Reza Abbasi

IEEE conference manuscript. Stationary 2.5 MW / 5 MWh BESS. Energy prices: SMARD DE-LU (CC BY 4.0). Reserve prices: published German aFRR four-hour block means, held fixed across every day. Every reported program is HiGHS kOptimal. Seed 20260821.

Abstract— This paper is a diagnostic, not a hunt for rare ranking flips. Three linear aging rows are swapped inside one mean–CVaR day-ahead energy and reserve LP: throughput, Xu daily depth, and reserve-aware layered calendar–cycle aging (ReLCCA). ReLCCA prices SoC-band occupancy and band flow, so parked upward reserve pays calendar cost. It is a linear encoding of published NMC fade, not a new cell model. The plant is a price-taker on SMARD DE-LU (1 September 2025 – 21 August 2026, with a gap 21 October – 30 November 2025). Capacity prices are the same published 2025–mid-2026 aFRR four-hour block means on every day; they are not the 1 May auction tape. A controlled injection of that midday trough onto three residuals (19 August, 24 May, 5 April) locates the condition: occupancy and cycle-depth stop being substitutes only if the energy-only book would otherwise go long into the trough. On this plant that condition appears once in the extract (the recorded 1 May 2026 path). The same −499 EUR/MWh midday on the two neighbour residuals leaves throughput reserve-positive. A scan of all 20 days with a minimum below −20 EUR/MWh finds no second case. The euro gap on that path is a different day-ahead energy bid, not the printed aging bill. The contribution is the procedure that isolates that mechanism.

Index Terms— Virtual power plant, CVaR, stationary battery energy storage, calendar aging, cycle-depth cost, aFRR, SMARD, linear programming.

I. Introduction

A virtual power plant (VPP) offers distributed resources to wholesale markets as one portfolio [1]. Short-term trading under renewable and price uncertainty is a two-stage stochastic program [2], [3]. Mid-term and day-ahead VPP models of that type are now standard [4], [5]. Price-maker variants become bi-level [6]; price-takers use robust and adaptive-robust offering [7], [8]. Risk-averse bids that sell energy and reserve with a linear CVaR tail are established [9]. Recent surveys keep the same two-stage skeleton [10]–[12].

Coherent risk measures [13] and the Rockafellar–Uryasev CVaR representation [14], [15] put the tail inside an LP. Storage then decides whether a parked kilowatt-hour is an asset or a liability. Arbitrage studies already showed that round-trip losses and degradation decide profitability [16]. Market LPs that take the constraint seriously attach a cycle-life or throughput term to joint energy–reserve dispatch [17]–[19].

Lithium-ion cells also age at rest. Vetter et al. and Broussely et al. separated calendar and cycle fade [20], [21]. Barré et al. reviewed the split [22]. Keil et al. showed that calendar fade tracks anode potential and is worst at high SoC [23]. Schmalstieg et al. and Ecker et al. gave semi-empirical NMC laws in which calendar fade grows with voltage and time, typically as √t [24], [25]. Market encodings remain cycle-centric: Xu et al. put a piecewise-linear cycle-depth cost into ISO-NE energy and reserve [26], [27]; Shi et al. proved rainflow cycle cost is convex [28]; Wankmüller et al. quantified the revenue effect [29]. Those models price current. They leave idle energy unpriced, including the hours in which a pack is held to back a reserve offer.

The question in this paper is diagnostic: when does pricing that idle energy change a mean–CVaR energy–reserve bid relative to a cycle-depth row? The method is one LP, three interchangeable aging rows, a wholesale decompose that separates the aging bill from the day-ahead position, and a controlled trough injection that varies only the midday price path. The SMARD extract is the test bed, not the claim. Reserve capacity prices used here are published German aFRR four-hour block means for 2025–mid-2026 [30], [31], applied unchanged to every operating day. They are not the cleared auction of any particular day.

Contributions:

1) A same-plant diagnostic that isolates when occupancy and cycle-depth stop being substitutes: freeze the residual plant, inject a scaled negative trough, and read the sign of reserve minus energy-only realised euro.

2) A linear occupancy row (ReLCCA) that already contains both calendar and cycle terms, with the reserve coupling restricted to parking. Calendar-only and cycle-only ablations, band-count and κ-assignment checks sit in the same LP.

3) An application to SMARD DE-LU in which the diagnosed condition appears once. The paper reports that count and does not generalise it to a price threshold.

Section II places the rows. Section III is the plant, the scale factors and the imbalance proxy. Section IV writes the LPs and the three-plateau κ map. Section V is the bid. Section VI is the diagnostic protocol. Section VII reports numbers. Section VIII discusses the mechanism. Section IX lists limits. Section X closes.

II. Related Work

Day-ahead VPP bids are two-stage [2]–[5], [9]. This plant is a price-taker, so bi-level offering [6] is unused. CVaR [14], [15] hedges the tail once a distribution is assumed. Wasserstein VPP bids [32], [33] are left as related work. Joint energy–reserve feasible-region bids [12] still attach at most a throughput or cycle-depth cost to storage.

Xu et al. replace throughput by a piecewise-linear function of cycle depth [26], [27]. Shi et al. prove rainflow convexity [28]. Semi-empirical NMC laws already add calendar and cycle fade [24], [25]; market LPs almost never carry both into the reserve row. ReLCCA puts both terms in (1) and couples only occupancy to parked upward reserve through (2). It does not price aFRR activation energy. Table I is the map.

Imbalance is a two-price function of the day-ahead price: short = DA + 18 + 0.12|DA|, long = DA − 10 − 0.08|DA|, with short ≥ DA+5 and long ≤ DA−3. The additive 10–18 EUR/MWh and the 8–12% terms are rounded values in the range of typical German imbalance premia when a reBAP series is not available. They are not estimated from 2026 balancing energy. Clipping |DA| at 150 EUR/MWh inside that formula is a robustness check, not a calibration.

IV. Three Aging Laws

Throughput attaches 1.5 EUR/MWh to charge and to discharge. Xu daily depth is dω = (maxt Et,ω − mint Et,ω)/Eb, written as a convex PWL with widths (0.20, 0.30, 0.30, 0.20) and slopes (10, 40, 80, 120) EUR per unit depth [26]. A full nameplate swing costs 62 EUR before throughput.

ReLCCA partitions [Emin, Emax] into N bands of equal width. Fill fractions obey ut,ω,ℓ ≤ ut,ω,ℓ−1. Calendar and cycle costs are

Cωcal = Σt,ℓ κℓ ΔEℓ ut,ω,ℓ,   Cωcyc = Σt,ℓ (γℓch pt,ω,ℓch + γℓdis pt,ω,ℓdis). (1)

Upward reserve occupies those bands:

rt↑ Δt/ηd ≤ Σℓ ut,ω,ℓ ΔEℓ. (2)

Equation (1) is already combined calendar-plus-cycle cost. Constraint (2) prices parking, not activated reserve energy. The default band count is N = 8; N ∈ {4, 6, 8, 12} is solved on 1 May.

Calendar coefficients come from three published Keil NMC 25 °C ten-month plateaus [23]: 1.2% fade at low SoC, 2.4% at mid SoC, 4.0% at high SoC. Linear 12/10 annualisation times 150 000 EUR/MWh replacement converts those three numbers into κ ∈ {0.247, 0.493, 0.822} EUR/MWh/h. The eight bands of the default map are a step assignment of those three values (low, low, mid, mid, mid, high, high, high). A quadratic through the eight assigned centres has R² = 0.89; that figure is a smoother of a three-level step, not a statistical fit on independent cells, and is not used as evidence. Uncertainty is reported by re-solving 1 May with all-mid κ, all-high κ, and a 5.5% top-band (Keil at 100% SoC). Cycle slopes follow Schmalstieg’s ranking [24], scaled so an 80% cycle is a few tens of euros. Fig. 3 is the three-plateau step. The cascade that those coefficients sit on is drawn next.

Bands fill from the bottom. Band ℓ cannot hold energy unless band ℓ−1 is full, so the occupied energy in (2) is a contiguous stack. Fig. 4 is that stack on the 10–90% window.

V. Mean–CVaR Bid

The three aging rows sit in one two-stage LP. For every hour t and scenario ω the power balance is

pDAt + pcht,ω + ilongt,ω − pdist,ω − pDGt,ω − ishortt,ω = PVt,ω + Wt,ω − Lt,ω. (3)

Battery energy follows

Et,ω = Et−1,ω + ηc pcht,ω − pdist,ω/ηd,   E23,ω = Einit, (4)

with E in [0.1, 0.9] Eb. Diesel respects a 1.2 MW/h ramp. Inverter headroom keeps r↑ and r↓ inside the remaining charge and discharge capacity. Scenario profit is

Πω = Σt (λDAt,ω pDAt + λ↑t r↑t + λ↓t r↓t + λlongt,ω ilongt,ω − λshortt,ω ishortt,ω − 68 pDGt,ω) − Cωage, (5)

where Cωage is the throughput row, the Xu PWL, or Cωcal+Cωcyc from (1). The offering objective is

max   (1−β) Σω πω Πω + β CVaRα(Π), (6)

with the Rockafellar–Uryasev cone zω ≥ η − Πω, zω ≥ 0 [14]. Settlement freezes pDA, r↑, r↓ and re-solves the same aging law on the realised path. A 180 EUR/MWh load-service overlay is added after the solve for reporting. Rankings do not depend on the overlay. Wholesale components are Table V.

VI. Diagnostic Protocol

The protocol has four steps. (i) Solve the three rows on ordinary winter, spring and summer days and on two rolling weeks. If they agree, occupancy is not moving the ranking on those residuals. (ii) Freeze a residual plant and replace hours 9–16 by a scaled copy of the 1 May trough. Repeat on 19 August, 24 May and 5 April. Read the sign of P1−B2. That curve says whether a price trough alone is enough. (iii) Decompose wholesale profit on any day where the sign changes, to separate the aging bill from the day-ahead position. (iv) Scan every real day whose minimum is below −20 EUR/MWh, and repeat the sign-changing day under N, κ assignment, calendar/cycle split, K, √t annualisation and an imbalance clip. Seed 20260821.

B. Controlled trough injection

This is the core experiment. Hours 9–16 of 19 August are replaced by a scaled copy of the 1 May trough. Load, PV, wind and the aFRR block schedule stay at 19 August. The injected minimum runs from −50 to −499 EUR/MWh. Table IV and Fig. 7 report P1−B2. Throughput falls from +1 145 to +790 EUR and never crosses zero. ReLCCA stays above +1 050 EUR at every depth. A price trough of −499 EUR/MWh is therefore not sufficient to reverse the ranking on this residual (load 85 MWh, net 56 MWh). The same midday hours were written onto 24 May (net 32 MWh) and 5 April (net 28 MWh), the two residuals closest in daily net to 1 May. At an injected minimum of −499, throughput remains +802 and +919 EUR. Occupancy matters when the energy-only book would go long into the trough. Neither neighbour residual produces that book.

VIII. Discussion

The diagnostic answers a when-question. Occupancy and cycle-depth agree while the energy-only book stays short. They disagree when that book would go long into a deep negative window, because the occupancy cascade and the parking inequality change the feasible day-ahead position by about a thousand euros, while the printed aging bill stays in the tens. The injection shows that the trough depth alone does not create that long book. Writing the same midday onto 24 May and 5 April — residuals whose daily net matches 1 May to within 3 MWh — still leaves throughput reserve-positive at −499 EUR/MWh. The recorded 1 May path is a joint residual-and-price event. The twenty-day scan shows that this extract contains one such event.

That count is a property of the panel, not a defect of the protocol. A larger panel, or a cleared aFRR tape that co-moves with solar glut, would be a new application of the same four steps. It would not be a different method.

ReLCCA is a linear occupancy encoding inside an existing CVaR bid. Splitting (1) into calendar-only and cycle-only does not restore the throughput ranking on 1 May. The reserve coupling is parking. Activated aFRR energy is not in the LP. The aFRR prices themselves are annual block means. Those three facts bound what the 1 May illustration can be said to show: a change in the energy bid under a fixed reserve schedule, not a replay of the 1 May auction.

IX. Limitations

The diagnosed condition appears once in 312 complete days. A week in October 2025 is solved and agrees; 21 October – 30 November 2025 is a fetch gap. κ is a three-plateau conversion of Keil 2016, assigned to N bands; it is not a Sandia/NASA cell file and the quadratic R² on those assigned centres is not a confidence interval. Linear 12/10 annualisation ignores the √t calendar law in [24], [25]; the 0.913 rescaling leaves the 1 May sign unchanged. 150 EUR/kWh is cell replacement, not installed EPC. aFRR prices are the same published block means on every day [30], [31]. reBAP is not mounted. The two-price imbalance formula and the three frozen scale factors are proxies, documented in Section III. Xu is daily-depth PWL, not Shi rainflow. Isolation uses K = 30; the injection and the scan use K = 20; signs match at both K on 5 April, 26 April, 1 May and 24 May. Two ReLCCA energy-only first stages (26 April, 2 May) require the HiGHS interior-point fallback. The reproduction package is the local archive Hassanli_Abbasi_Occupancy_Aging_PACKAGE.tgz (source, SMARD extracts, recorded CSV, and this manuscript). A public GitHub or Zenodo deposit is not attached.

X. Conclusion

A four-step diagnostic was run on a SMARD price-taker with three linear aging rows in one CVaR energy–reserve LP. Ordinary days agree. Injecting a −499 EUR/MWh midday onto 19 August, 24 May and 5 April does not reverse throughput. The recorded 1 May path does, because that energy-only book goes long and the occupancy row does not. A twenty-day scan finds no second case. The euro gap is a different day-ahead bid. Reserve prices are annual aFRR block means, not the auction of that day. The method is the contribution. The single path is the illustration.
