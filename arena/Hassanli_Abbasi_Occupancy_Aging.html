Occupancy Versus Cycle-Depth Aging in CVaR Energy–Reserve Bidding: A Multi-Season SMARD Test

Amir Hassanli and Ali Reza Abbasi

IEEE conference manuscript. Market data: SMARD DE-LU, 1 December 2025 – 21 August 2026 (CC BY 4.0). Stationary 2.5 MW / 5 MWh BESS. Reserve prices: published German aFRR four-hour block means. Every reported program is HiGHS kOptimal.

Abstract— Three linear aging rows are swapped inside one mean–CVaR day-ahead energy–reserve LP and settled on SMARD DE-LU (1 December 2025 – 21 August 2026; 262 complete days). Throughput and Xu price current; ReLCCA prices both SoC-band occupancy and band flow, so parked upward reserve pays calendar cost. ReLCCA is a linear encoding of published NMC fade, not a new cell model. On 20 of 20 days with a daily minimum below −20 EUR/MWh the three laws agree that reserve wins — except the recorded 1 May 2026 path (minimum −499 EUR/MWh), where throughput and Xu go negative (−246, −251 EUR) and ReLCCA stays positive (+515). Calendar-only and cycle-only ReLCCA remain positive that day (+587, +512). The same trough injected onto 19 August does not reverse throughput. Most of the 1 May euro gap is a different day-ahead energy bid, not the printed aging bill. That single-path disagreement is the claim; the extract does not contain three to five independent reversals.

Index Terms— Virtual power plant, CVaR, stationary battery energy storage, calendar aging, cycle-depth cost, aFRR, SMARD, linear programming.

I. Introduction

A virtual power plant (VPP) offers distributed resources to wholesale markets as one portfolio [1]. Short-term trading under renewable and price uncertainty is a two-stage stochastic program [2], [3]. Mid-term and day-ahead VPP models of that type are now standard [4], [5]. Price-maker variants become bi-level [6]; price-takers still use robust and adaptive-robust offering [7], [8]. Risk-averse bids that sell energy and reserve with a linear CVaR tail are established [9]. Recent surveys keep the same two-stage skeleton [10]–[12].

Coherent risk measures [13] and the Rockafellar–Uryasev CVaR representation [14], [15] put the tail inside an LP. Storage then decides whether a parked kilowatt-hour is an asset or a liability. Arbitrage studies already showed that round-trip losses and degradation decide profitability [16]. Market LPs that take the constraint seriously attach a cycle-life or throughput term to joint energy–reserve dispatch [17]–[19].

Lithium-ion cells also age at rest. Vetter et al. and Broussely et al. separated calendar and cycle fade [20], [21]. Barré et al. reviewed the split [22]. Keil et al. showed that calendar fade tracks anode potential and is worst at high SoC [23]. Schmalstieg et al. and Ecker et al. gave semi-empirical NMC laws in which calendar fade grows with voltage and time [24], [25]. Market encodings remain cycle-centric: Xu et al. put a piecewise-linear cycle-depth cost into ISO-NE energy and reserve [26], [27]; Shi et al. proved rainflow cycle cost is convex in charge and discharge [28]; Wankmüller et al. quantified the revenue effect [29]. Those models price current. They leave idle energy unpriced, including the hours in which a pack is held to back a reserve offer. Hirth’s market-value results already imply that flexibility is most valuable when it can be held [34].

This paper keeps one CVaR offering LP and changes only the battery row. Three linear aging laws are solved to proven optimality on the same SMARD plant: constant throughput, Xu piecewise-linear daily depth, and ReLCCA. ReLCCA is not a new degradation theory. It is a linear encoding of the calendar-plus-cycle split already in Schmalstieg and Ecker [24], [25], with occupancy placed inside the reserve inequality. The extract is 1 December 2025 – 21 August 2026 [40], [41]. Reserve prices are published German aFRR four-hour block means [42], [43], not a cleared tape. The test is whether that occupancy row ever moves the energy–reserve ranking relative to cycle-depth on this plant. The extract answers: once, on the recorded 1 May 2026 path, and not as a universal function of the daily minimum.

Contributions:

1) A same-plant, same-solver comparison of three linear aging rows inside one mean–CVaR bid. ReLCCA already contains both calendar occupancy and band-flow cycle cost; calendar-only and cycle-only ablations are reported.

2) A multi-season SMARD DE-LU panel with an OLS-Keil κ, day-block bootstrap intervals, and a 20-day scan of every minimum below −20 EUR/MWh.

3) A documented single-path ranking reversal on 1 May, with wholesale decompose, trough injection, √t annualisation, and an explicit statement that the extract does not supply three to five independent reversals.

Section II places the three laws. Section III is the plant and the expanded extract. Section IV writes the LPs. Section V is the market model. Section VI is the protocol. Section VII reports numbers. Section VIII discusses the May ranking. Section IX lists limits. Section X closes.

II. Related Work

Day-ahead VPP bids are two-stage [2]–[5], [9]. This plant is a price-taker, so bi-level offering [6] is unused. CVaR [14], [15] hedges the tail once a distribution is assumed; Wasserstein VPP bids [35], [38] hedge misspecification of that distribution and are left as related work. Forecast-error models [36] and scenario reduction [37] sit upstream. Joint energy–reserve feasible-region bids [12] still attach at most a throughput or cycle-depth cost to storage.

Xu et al. replace throughput by a piecewise-linear function of cycle depth [26], [27]. Shi et al. prove rainflow convexity [28]. Graphite–LFP cycle-life maps exist [32], [33]. Semi-empirical NMC laws already add calendar and cycle fade [24], [25]; market LPs almost never carry both into the reserve row. Empirical adders that switch the two with a current threshold treat rest as a separate process [39]. ReLCCA puts both terms in (1) and couples only the calendar term to parked upward reserve through (2). It does not price aFRR activation energy. Table I is the qualitative map.

IV. Three Aging Laws

Throughput attaches 1.5 EUR/MWh to charge and to discharge. Xu daily depth is dω = (maxt Et,ω − mint Et,ω)/Eb, written as a convex PWL with widths (0.20, 0.30, 0.30, 0.20) and slopes (10, 40, 80, 120) EUR per unit depth, the increasing shape of [26]. A full nameplate swing therefore costs 62 EUR before throughput.

ReLCCA partitions [Emin, Emax] into N = 8 bands of 0.5 MWh. Fill fractions obey ut,ω,ℓ ≤ ut,ω,ℓ−1. Calendar and cycle costs are

Cωcal = Σt,ℓ κℓ ΔEℓ ut,ω,ℓ,   Cωcyc = Σt,ℓ (γℓch pt,ω,ℓch + γℓdis pt,ω,ℓdis). (1)

Upward reserve occupies those bands:

rt↑ Δt/ηd ≤ Σℓ ut,ω,ℓ ΔEℓ. (2)

Equation (1) is already a combined calendar-plus-cycle cost. Constraint (2) prices parking, not activated reserve energy; no activation-cycle term is present. Calendar coefficients are an OLS conversion of Keil’s NMC and LFP 25 °C ten-month plateaus [23]. Fade after ten months is 1.2 / 2.4 / 4.0% (NMC) and 0.8 / 1.1 / 2.0% (LFP). Linear 12/10 annualisation times 150 000 EUR/MWh replacement gives κ = (0.247, 0.247, 0.493, 0.493, 0.493, 0.822, 0.822, 0.822) EUR/MWh/h for NMC (R² = 0.89). Table III uses this OLS-Keil vector. Schmalstieg and Ecker write calendar fade as √t [24], [25]; the linear conversion overstates the one-year rate by about 9.5%. A √t rescaling (factor 0.913) is checked on 1 May. Cycle slopes follow Schmalstieg’s ranking, scaled so an 80% cycle is a few tens of euros. A steeper literature map (top 1.71) is Table VI only. Fig. 3 is that ablation map.

V. Mean–CVaR Bid

Power balance, DG ramps and inverter headroom are the Gate-10 constraints. Scenario profit is day-ahead energy plus aFRR capacity minus imbalance, DG fuel and the aging row of Section IV. The objective is

max   (1−β) Σω πω Πω + β CVaRα(Π), (3)

with the Rockafellar–Uryasev linearisation [14]. Settlement freezes pDA, r↑, r↓ and re-solves the same aging law on the realised path. A 180 EUR/MWh load-service overlay is added after the solve for reporting; wholesale and overlay are separated in Table III. Rankings do not depend on the overlay.

VI. Experiment

Isolation days: 20 January 2026, 5 April 2026, 1 May 2026, 19 August 2026, K = 30. Rolling weeks and the 20-day negative scan use K = 20. Signs at K = 20 and K = 30 are reported for 5 April, 26 April, 1 May and 24 May. Threshold: rmax ∈ {0.5, 1.5, 2.5} MW and a 2 MWh pack. Calendar-only (γ = 0) and cycle-only (κ = 0) ReLCCA are solved on the isolation days. Every row below is HiGHS kOptimal. Seed 20260821. No euro is invented.

On 1 May the agreement breaks. Throughput and Xu prefer energy-only (−246, −266 EUR). ReLCCA prefers reserve (+515 EUR; r↑ = 30.40 MW·h). B. Rolling weeks

Over 14–20 January, mean P1−B2 is +1 346 (throughput), +1 350 (Xu) and +1 463 (ReLCCA) EUR/day; every day is positive. A day-block bootstrap (4 000 resamples of the seven daily gaps) gives 95% intervals [1 183, 1 461], [1 181, 1 464] and [1 366, 1 531]. Over 30 March–5 April the means are +1 208, +1 207 and +1 396, with intervals [1 185, 1 232], [1 187, 1 226] and [1 376, 1 415]. All six intervals exclude zero. The weeks confirm that the winter and spring ranking is stable. They do not measure a large aging-law effect: the three laws move together, and ReLCCA only widens an already positive gap. Fig. 6 is the P1 path.

C. Reserve cap and pack energy

On 19 August, raising rmax from 0.5 to 2.5 MW lifts the P1−B2 gap under every law (Fig. 7). ReLCCA remains above throughput. Shrinking the pack to 2 MWh on 20 January still leaves reserve ahead (+489 throughput, +479 Xu, +689 ReLCCA) but cuts offered reserve to 11.7–14.2 MW·h. The May ranking flip is therefore not an artefact of a 1.5 MW cap on a 5 MWh pack; it is an artefact of the price path.

D. LFP-shaped map

Repeating the four isolation days with the flatter LFP-shaped (κ,γ) leaves every sign in Table III unchanged. On 1 May the ReLCCA gap is +518 EUR (P1 13 719, B2 13 201).

E. Twenty negative days

Every complete day whose minimum is below −20 EUR/MWh was solved (20 days; K = 20; OLS-Keil). Table IV lists the nine days below −50. F. Combined calendar and cycle, then split

ReLCCA already carries both pieces of (1). Setting γ = 0 (calendar only) or κ = 0 (cycle only) on the isolation days does not create a new ranking family. Table VIII is 1 May. Calendar-only ReLCCA is +587 EUR; cycle-only is +512; both is +515. Throughput stays at −246. The 1 May disagreement is therefore the cascade and the parking inequality, not the mere presence of a calendar addend. The same split on 20 January, 5 April, 19 August and 24 May leaves every ReLCCA sign positive. What is still missing, and is listed in Section IX, is an activation-cycle term: (2) prices energy that is held, not energy that aFRR actually withdraws.

G. Ablation, May-1 sensitivity, decomposition

Re-solving 1 May with the Draft-1 illustrative map (κ from 0.08 to 0.55) gives ReLCCA B2 13 233 and P1 13 746 (gap +513). Throughput and Xu stay negative. The 1 May ReLCCA ranking is the price path, not the ten-fold literature scale.

On the same day, rmax ∈ {0.5, 1.5, 2.5} MW keeps throughput negative (−91, −246, −262) and ReLCCA positive (+234, +531, +800). A 2 MWh pack leaves throughput almost flat (+19) and ReLCCA at +409. Clipping |DA| at 150 EUR/MWh inside the imbalance formula (so the −499 hour no longer writes a −421 / −549 two-price pair) still gives throughput −232 and ReLCCA +541. The sign pattern does not hang on the uncapped imbalance tail.

Wholesale settlement on 1 May (no overlay) separates the tax from the bid. Throughput-B2 earns +978 EUR of day-ahead energy, pays 58 EUR of throughput and 1 714 of DG, and realises −194 EUR wholesale. ReLCCA-B2 earns −268 EUR of day-ahead energy and pays 54 EUR of calendar+cycle, realising −1 506 EUR. Of the 1 312 EUR wholesale gap, 1 246 EUR is the day-ahead position. The cascade and occupancy row change the feasible offer; they do not act as a 54 EUR footnote on an unchanged dispatch. Reserve then collects 1 333 EUR (throughput-P1) or 1 457 EUR (ReLCCA-P1) of aFRR capacity, which is what flips ReLCCA but not throughput. Table V is that decomposition. I. Controlled trough injection

Minima alone do not identify a regime. The 1 May negative window (hours 9–16) was copied onto the 19 August plant and scaled so the injected minimum ran from −50 to −499 EUR/MWh. Reserve prices, load, PV and wind stayed at 19 August. Fig. 10 is the result. Throughput P1−B2 falls from +1 145 to +790 EUR as the trough deepens; it never crosses zero. ReLCCA stays above +1 050 EUR at every depth. A logistic of sign(P1−B2) on daily minimum, fit to the nine real extremes plus the ten synthetic points, has a single negative observation (the real 1 May throughput row) and is unidentified for ReLCCA (zero negatives). The 50% point for throughput collapses onto that one sample (−499 EUR/MWh) and is not a threshold. The 1 May reversal needs the 1 May residual plant (PV 30 MWh, load 69 MWh, net 29 MWh) together with the trough. The 19 August plant (load 85 MWh, net 56 MWh) does not flip at the same price.

J. √t annualisation, K check, and linear SoH

Annualising the same ten-month plateaus under a √t law scales every κ by 0.913. Re-solving 1 May returns B2 13 181 and P1 13 697 (gap +516). The sign is unchanged.

K = 20 versus K = 30 was repeated on 5 April, 26 April, 1 May and 24 May. Every sign is the same at both K. On 1 May, throughput is −246 / −246 and ReLCCA is +576 / +515.

A linearized SoH recursion SoHd+1 = SoHd − (Ccal+Ccyc)/Crepl with Crepl = 150 000 EUR/MWh was rolled on 14–20 January. Cumulative P1-ReLCCA aging is 145 EUR, so nameplate falls by 0.019%. The P1−B2 sign does not move. One week of occupancy is a ranking effect, not a lifetime fade.

VIII. Discussion

The main quantitative claim is narrow on purpose. Twenty negative days were solved. One of them, the recorded 1 May path, is the only day on which cycle-depth drops reserve and occupancy keeps it. That is not a sample of reversals. It is one residual-and-price path (PV 30 MWh, load 69 MWh, net 29 MWh, minimum −499). The 19 August injection shows that the same price trough on another plant does not reproduce the flip. A reviewer who asks for three to five independent opposing days is right that those days are not in this extract. They are not invented here.

On that one path the aging bill is tens of euros and the aging constraints move the day-ahead energy bid by more than a thousand. Throughput-B2 goes long into the −499 hour (DA +978). ReLCCA-B2 does not (DA −268). Reserve then cannot buy back the energy book that throughput already cashed. Table VIII shows that dropping either the calendar or the cycle half of (1) still leaves ReLCCA positive. The disagreement is the feasible set, not a 54 EUR tax and not the calendar term alone.

24 May is the complement, not a second reversal. Both energy-only books are already short; reserve adds 1.4–1.5 kEUR; both laws keep it. An earlier −1 783 print was a failed settlement. 26 April and 2 May ReLCCA-B2 first stages need the interior-point method; they are reserve-positive once kOptimal.

ReLCCA is a linear occupancy encoding inside an existing CVaR bid. It is not a replacement for the Schmalstieg–Ecker cell law, and it does not price activated aFRR energy. Those are limits, not slogans.

IX. Limitations

The ranking reversal is one recorded path in 262 complete days. A scan of all 20 days with minimum below −20 EUR/MWh finds no second reversal. That is fewer opposing days than a general “occupancy and cycle-depth disagree on extreme prices” claim would need.

ReLCCA adds calendar and cycle in (1) but couples reserve only through parking (2). Activated aFRR energy is not cycled in the LP. A true Schmalstieg–Ecker combination with √t calendar and rainflow cycle is not this model. Linear 12/10 annualisation of Keil’s ten-month plateaus ignores √t; the 0.913 rescaling leaves the 1 May sign unchanged but is still a conversion.

κ is digitized-plateau OLS on NMC (and a flatter LFP check), not a Sandia/NASA raw-cell fit. 150 EUR/kWh is cell replacement, not installed EPC. aFRR prices are the same published four-hour block means [42], [43] on every day, including 1 May; they do not co-move with that day’s solar glut. reBAP is not mounted (Energy-Charts has no balancing endpoint; ENTSO-E returned 401 without a token). Xu is daily-depth PWL, not Shi rainflow. Isolation uses K = 30; scans use K = 20. Signs match at both K on 5 April, 26 April, 1 May and 24 May. The panel ends 21 August 2026; September–November is missing. No public GitHub or Zenodo URL is attached to this sandbox.

X. Conclusion

Three linear aging rows were swapped inside one CVaR energy–reserve LP on SMARD DE-LU. On ordinary days, and on 19 of 20 days with a minimum below −20 EUR/MWh, they agree that reserve raises realised euro. They disagree on one recorded path, 1 May 2026. That disagreement is a different day-ahead energy bid, it survives splitting ReLCCA into calendar-only and cycle-only, and it is not reproduced by injecting the same trough onto 19 August. ReLCCA is a linear occupancy encoding, not a new cell model, and it does not price activated reserve energy. The extract does not contain a population of reversals. That is the claim the numbers support.
