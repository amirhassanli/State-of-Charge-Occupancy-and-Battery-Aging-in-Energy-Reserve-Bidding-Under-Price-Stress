When Occupancy Aging Changes a CVaR Energy–Reserve Bid: A SMARD Diagnostic

Amir Hassanli and Ali Reza Abbasi

Abstract—This paper develops and evaluates a diagnostic framework for determining when explicit calendar-aging exposure can change a stationary battery energy storage system (BESS) bidding decision relative to a cycle-centric degradation model. A price-taking virtual power plant (VPP) comprising a 2.5 MW/5 MWh BESS is formulated as a single linear mean–CVaR day-ahead energy and reserve optimization problem. Three interchangeable aging representations are evaluated under identical plant, market, and uncertainty conditions: a throughput cost, a piecewise-linear daily depth-of-discharge (DoD) model, and the proposed Reserve-aware Layered Calendar–Cycle Aging (ReLCCA) formulation. ReLCCA represents state-of-charge (SoC) occupancy and band flow explicitly, thereby assigning a calendar-aging cost to energy parked to support upward reserve. The formulation preserves linearity through band decomposition, piecewise-linear epigraphs, and the standard Rockafellar–Uryasev CVaR representation. Public German DE-LU market and physical series are used for a 262-day study window. The empirical analysis is deliberately diagnostic rather than a claim of universal dominance. On ordinary isolation days, the three aging laws produce closely aligned reserve-versus-energy rankings. On 1 May 2026, however, the throughput energy-only solution becomes long during a −499 EUR/MWh midday trough, whereas ReLCCA remains short. Wholesale decomposition shows that the resulting 1,312 EUR difference is dominated by the day-ahead energy position (1,246 EUR), while the direct aging-cost difference is only 54 EUR. A controlled trough-injection experiment and a 20-day negative-price scan further show that the mechanism is conditional on the residual portfolio state, not negative prices alone. The study therefore contributes a reproducible diagnostic procedure for identifying when calendar-aware BESS degradation can alter market positioning in a risk-aware VPP model.

Index Terms—Virtual power plant, battery energy storage, battery degradation, calendar aging, cycle-depth cost, CVaR, aFRR, SMARD, linear programming.

I. INTRODUCTION

Virtual power plants (VPPs) aggregate distributed energy resources into a coordinated market-facing portfolio, enabling heterogeneous assets to provide energy and ancillary services as a controllable unit [1], [2]. The economic value of such portfolios is strongly affected by uncertainty in renewable production and electricity prices, which has motivated stochastic, robust, and risk-aware bidding formulations [3]–[9]. In these models, stationary battery energy storage systems (BESSs) provide temporal flexibility and reserve capability, but their value cannot be assessed independently of the degradation caused by operation and by time spent at particular states of charge (SoC).

The battery-degradation literature distinguishes cycle aging from calendar aging. Established experimental and semi-empirical studies show that capacity loss depends on factors including depth of discharge, SoC, temperature, time, and electrochemical state [20]–[25]. Market-oriented formulations have consequently moved from simple throughput penalties toward cycle-depth functions and degradation-aware dispatch [17], [18], [26]–[29]. Nevertheless, the market optimization literature often represents degradation as a cost of charging/discharging or cycle depth. Such representations can price energy throughput while leaving the economic exposure of energy deliberately held in the battery insufficiently explicit.

This issue matters when BESS capacity is simultaneously used for energy arbitrage and reserve. Upward reserve requires energy headroom and therefore constrains the SoC region in which the battery must remain. If calendar aging depends on SoC, then the energy parked to preserve reserve availability has an opportunity cost that is not equivalent to a pure throughput charge. Existing work on energy–reserve co-optimization, battery degradation, and probabilistic reserve delivery provides important building blocks [18], [19], [30]–[33], but the interaction between SoC occupancy, reserve parking, and calendar aging remains comparatively underexplored in a transparent linear market model.

This paper addresses that issue with a deliberately narrow research question: under what residual-load and price conditions does explicitly pricing SoC occupancy change the optimal mean–CVaR energy–reserve bid relative to a cycle-depth representation? The objective is not to establish that one degradation model dominates another under all conditions. Instead, the study constructs a controlled diagnostic in which the plant, uncertainty treatment, solver, and market assumptions remain fixed while only the aging representation changes.

The main contributions are fourfold. First, a unified linear formulation permits three aging laws to be swapped without changing the remaining market model. Second, ReLCCA combines a calendar-aging occupancy term and a cycle-aging band-flow term and couples upward reserve to stored-energy parking. Third, a wholesale decomposition separates the direct aging-cost difference from changes in the optimized day-ahead energy position. Fourth, a controlled negative-price injection and an exhaustive scan of all qualifying days in the study window identify whether the observed ranking reversal is a generic price effect or a conditional portfolio-state effect.

The remainder of the paper is organized as follows. Section II reviews VPP bidding, battery-aging, risk, and reserve-market literature. Section III defines the system, data, and information structure. Section IV presents the complete linear mathematical formulation. Section V describes the experimental design. Section VI reports the results. Section VII discusses the mechanism and robustness. Section VIII states limitations, and Section IX concludes.

II. RELATED WORK AND RESEARCH GAP

The literature review was constructed from the supplied Literature Review document and the manuscript reference base, with emphasis on studies directly relevant to the intersection of VPP market participation, BESS degradation, uncertainty, risk, and reserve services. General VPP surveys establish the role of aggregation and market participation [1], [10], [11]. More recent work has expanded the scope toward uncertainty-aware dispatch, joint energy–reserve scheduling, and risk-averse VPP operation [9], [30]–[34].

For market scheduling, stochastic and robust optimization are well established. Krishnamurthy et al. model storage arbitrage under day-ahead and real-time price uncertainty [19], while Baringo and Rahimiyan and Baringo and Baringo develop robust and adaptive-robust VPP offering formulations [7], [8]. Vahedipour-Dahraie et al. explicitly combine energy and reserve scheduling with demand response under risk aversion [9]. More recent studies extend this direction to distributionally robust VPP bidding [34], [35] and risk-averse energy–frequency regulation scheduling [36]. These studies demonstrate the importance of uncertainty treatment but do not resolve how calendar-aging exposure from reserve parking should be represented in a linear market model.

Battery-aging research provides the second foundation. Vetter et al. and Broussely et al. distinguish the major electrochemical aging mechanisms [20], [21], while Barré et al. review the dependence of aging on operating conditions [22]. Keil et al. demonstrate the importance of SoC for calendar aging [23], and Schmalstieg et al. provide a holistic semi-empirical NMC aging model combining calendar and cycle effects [24]. Ecker et al. provide experimental evidence for calendar and cycle life of NMC cells [25]. Naumann et al. provide complementary evidence for calendar aging of commercial LFP/graphite cells [37].

Optimization-oriented degradation models seek computational tractability. Hesse et al. formulate age- and efficiency-aware battery dispatch with MILP [17]. Xu et al. introduce cycle-aging cost functions suitable for electricity-market optimization [26], [27], and Shi et al. establish convexity properties of cycle-aging costs under rainflow-based formulations [28]. Collath et al. review aging-aware operation and subsequently demonstrate lifetime-profitability improvements from including calendar and cycle aging in optimization [18], [38]. Petrin et al. study convex-hull representations of battery aging and quantify the accuracy–computational-cost trade-off [39]. Kumtepeli et al. integrate electro-thermal and semi-empirical aging models into an optimization framework [40].

For ancillary services, He et al. incorporate performance-based regulation and cycle life into battery bidding [18], while Akhavan-Hejazi and Mohsenian-Rad study joint energy and reserve operation of storage under high wind penetration [29]. Toubeau et al. impose probabilistic guarantees on real-time reserve delivery [30]. Merten et al. study battery bidding in the German secondary control reserve market and the prediction of aFRR market variables [31], [32]. Fernández-Muñoz and Pérez-Díaz develop stochastic scheduling and redispatch models for a wind–battery VPP participating in day-ahead and secondary regulation markets, including degradation and storage opportunity cost [33], [41]. Ghanaee et al. more recently include both calendar and cycle degradation in day-ahead and reserve scheduling of a hybrid wind–battery plant [42].

Risk management is the third strand. Coherent risk measures were formalized by Artzner et al. [13], and the Rockafellar–Uryasev representation makes CVaR tractable within linear optimization [14], [15]. Recent VPP studies continue to use CVaR for energy and ancillary-service bidding [36], [43]. Onsomu et al. demonstrate uncertainty-aware VPP dispatch using CVaR-related formulations [44], while Wang et al. develop a CVaR-based energy and frequency-regulation bidding framework for PV-storage systems [43].

The literature therefore contains strong and complementary solutions to VPP aggregation, stochastic bidding, risk management, cycle-aging valuation, and reserve participation. The unresolved issue targeted here is narrower: a transparent linear formulation in which (i) calendar aging is linked to SoC occupancy, (ii) cycle aging remains explicitly represented, (iii) upward reserve is coupled to the energy parked in the battery, (iv) mean–CVaR bidding is retained, and (v) the mechanism is diagnosed on public German market data without claiming that proxy reserve or imbalance series are equivalent to day-specific cleared settlement tapes. The present study addresses this gap as a diagnostic rather than as a universal degradation-model validation.

III. SYSTEM, DATA, AND INFORMATION STRUCTURE

A. VPP Configuration and Market Role

The VPP is modeled as a price-taker in the German DE-LU bidding area. The portfolio contains a 5 MW-class industrial load, 4 MW PV, 2 MW wind, a 2.5 MW/5 MWh stationary BESS, and a 1.8 MW dispatchable generator. The battery uses ηc = ηd = 0.95, a 10–90% SoC range, and cyclic operation over each daily horizon. The DG marginal operating cost is 68 EUR/MWh and its ramp limit is 1.2 MW/h. The default upward/downward reserve cap is 1.5 MW. These values are the fixed study configuration and are not fitted to the observed results.

Table II separates physical assumptions from market-data proxies. In particular, reserve capacity prices are not day-specific auction tapes. The study uses published German aFRR four-hour block means for 2025–mid-2026 and applies the same six-block schedule to each operating day. Similarly, the imbalance price is represented by a documented two-price proxy because a directly mounted reBAP series was not available in the study package. These choices are retained explicitly to avoid presenting proxy values as observed settlements.

B. Public Data and Scaling

The empirical panel covers 1 December 2025 00:00 through 21 August 2026 07:00 Berlin time, yielding 6,319 hourly observations and 262 complete days. Public German market and physical series are obtained from Bundesnetzagentur SMARD through the Fraunhofer ISE Energy-Charts interface [45], [46]. The panel contains 403 negative-price hours. National physical series are scaled using the frozen Gate-10 factors so that the study-window 95th-percentile load is 5 MW and the window maxima of solar and wind correspond to 4 MW and 2 MW, respectively. The factors are not re-estimated on individual days.

The information structure distinguishes first-stage market positions from scenario-dependent operating recourse. For each day, day-ahead energy and reserve positions are common across scenarios, while charging, discharging, DG output, imbalance, and band-level operating variables respond to the realized scenario. Scenario probabilities are uniform within the daily scenario set.

C. Reserve and Imbalance Proxies

For each four-hour block, the reserve capacity prices are the published German aFRR block means used in the study. Positive-direction block means are 4.5, 12.0, 47.1, 18.4, 35.6, and 8.0 EUR/MW/h; negative-direction means are 6.0, 15.0, 36.9, 57.4, 20.0, and 2.4 EUR/MW/h [47], [48]. These values are held fixed across days. The imbalance proxy is defined by the day-ahead price λtDA: short deviations are settled at λtDA + 18 + 0.12|λtDA|, while long deviations are settled at λtDA − 10 − 0.08|λtDA|, with the additional bounds that the short price is at least λtDA + 5 and the long price is at most λtDA − 3. The 150 EUR/MWh clipping case is treated only as a robustness test.

IV. MATHEMATICAL FORMULATION

The three aging laws are embedded in the same daily linear market model. This separation is important: all non-aging constraints, market prices, scenario inputs, and risk parameters are unchanged when the aging law is replaced. The resulting optimization is a linear program (LP). No binary charging/discharging variable is required in the implemented formulation; simultaneous charge/discharge is prevented by the economic structure and the reported parameterization rather than by a binary mode constraint.

C. Scenario Power Balance

For every hour and scenario, the portfolio must balance physical load, scheduled market position, renewable generation, DG output, and battery exchange. With positive z representing a net deviation that is settled in the imbalance market, the balance is:

(4) pDA_t + g_tω + PV_tω + W_tω + pdis_tω − pch_tω + z_tω = L_tω, ∀t,ω.

Equation (4) is linear because every term is a scalar power variable or an exogenous parameter. The equation is the physical coupling between the first-stage day-ahead position and scenario-dependent real-time operation.

D. Renewable and DG Constraints

(5) 0 ≤ PV_tω ≤ PV̄_tω, 0 ≤ W_tω ≤ W̄_tω, ∀t,ω.

(6) 0 ≤ g_tω ≤ P̄DG, ∀t,ω.

(7) −R↓DG ≤ g_tω − g_(t−1)ω ≤ R↑DG, ∀t>0,ω.

The renewable limits are linear availability constraints. The DG ramp constraint is also linear and avoids any nonlinear unit-commitment representation.

E. BESS Energy and Power Constraints

(8) 0 ≤ pch_tω ≤ P̄B, 0 ≤ pdis_tω ≤ P̄B, ∀t,ω.

(9) E_tω = E_(t−1)ω + ηc pch_tω Δt − (pdis_tω Δt)/ηd, ∀t,ω.

(10) Emin ≤ E_tω ≤ Emax, ∀t,ω.

(11) E_0ω = E_Tω, ∀ω.

Equation (9) is linear because ηc, ηd, and Δt are fixed parameters. Equation (11) implements the cyclic daily boundary condition used in the study. The reported 10–90% SoC range is imposed through Emin = 0.1Emax and Emax,oper = 0.9Emax when the normalized SoC representation is used.

F. Reserve Feasibility and Parking

The reserve variables are first-stage commitments. They are limited by the battery power capability and by the energy that must remain available for upward deployment.

(12) 0 ≤ r_t↑ ≤ R̄, 0 ≤ r_t↓ ≤ R̄, ∀t.

(13) pdis_tω + r_t↑ ≤ P̄B, ∀t,ω.

(14) pch_tω + r_t↓ ≤ P̄B, ∀t,ω.

(15) r_t↑ Δt / ηd ≤ Σℓ ΔEℓ u_tωℓ, ∀t,ω.

Equation (15) is the reserve-parking constraint. It is linear because reserve capacity and occupancy are multiplied only by fixed parameters. Importantly, it prices the energy held to support upward reserve but does not claim to model the detailed energy of an activated aFRR signal. This distinction is central to the interpretation of the results.

G. SoC-Band Decomposition

For ReLCCA, the usable battery-energy interval [Emin,Emax] is divided into N equal-width bands. The occupancy variables satisfy:

(16) 0 ≤ u_tωℓ ≤ 1, ∀t,ω,ℓ.

(17) u_tωℓ ≤ u_tω,ℓ−1, ∀t,ω,ℓ>1.

(18) E_tω = Emin + Σℓ ΔEℓ u_tωℓ, ∀t,ω.

The monotonicity condition in (17) produces a cumulative-fill representation. Band-level flows are linked to occupancy changes through:

(19) ΔEℓ(u_tωℓ − u_(t−1)ωℓ) = ηc p_tωℓch Δt − p_tωℓdis Δt/ηd, ∀t,ω,ℓ.

(20) Σℓ p_tωℓch = p_tωch, Σℓ p_tωℓdis = p_tωdis, ∀t,ω.

Equations (16)–(20) are a linear discretization of the continuous SoC trajectory. The model does not claim that eight bands reproduce electrochemical aging exactly; N is a numerical resolution parameter and is therefore tested explicitly.

H. Throughput Aging Law

The throughput benchmark assigns a constant marginal aging cost to battery energy processed in either direction:

(21) Cωthr = Σt cthr (pch_tω + pdis_tω) Δt.

The source model uses cthr = 1.5 EUR/MWh. This formulation is linear but does not distinguish depth of discharge or calendar exposure. Idle energy is therefore unpriced by the aging term.

I. Xu Daily Cycle-Depth Law

The Xu benchmark represents daily depth as the range of stored energy normalized by battery capacity:

(22) dω ≥ (E_tω − E_kω)/Emax, ∀t,k,ω.

(23) dω ≥ (E_kω − E_tω)/Emax, ∀t,k,ω.

The absolute range is represented by the pair of linear inequalities (22)–(23). The daily depth cost is then approximated by a convex piecewise-linear function with break-widths 0.20, 0.30, 0.30, and 0.20 and slopes 10, 40, 80, and 120 EUR per unit depth [26]. Introducing nonnegative segment variables qωj gives:

(24) dω = Σj qωj, 0 ≤ qωj ≤ wj, CωXu = Σj mj qωj.

This is an epigraph/segment formulation of the convex piecewise-linear cost. No nonlinear product is introduced.

J. ReLCCA Calendar and Cycle Aging

ReLCCA adds an explicit cost to SoC occupancy and a separate cost to band-level energy flow:

(25) Cωcal = Σt Σℓ κℓ ΔEℓ u_tωℓ Δt.

(26) Cωcyc = Σt Σℓ (γℓch p_tωℓch + γℓdis p_tωℓdis) Δt.

(27) CωReLCCA = Cωcal + Cωcyc.

Equation (25) is the defining calendar-occupancy term. A battery that remains parked at a high-SoC band incurs a calendar-aging cost even if no charge/discharge energy is processed during that hour. Equation (26) retains a cycle-related cost based on band flow. Both equations are linear because κ, γ, ΔE, and Δt are fixed coefficients.

The default N = 8 bands use a three-plateau κ map derived from the supplied Keil-based calibration: low-SoC bands use 0.247 EUR/MWh/h, mid-SoC bands 0.493 EUR/MWh/h, and high-SoC bands 0.822 EUR/MWh/h. The values are a linear annualized conversion of the study's 10-month plateau assumptions and are not a new electrochemical fit. The model therefore treats the κ map as a scenario parameter rather than a statistically estimated cell-aging law.

K. Scenario Profit and Mean–CVaR Objective

For each scenario, wholesale profit is defined as:

(28) Πω = Σt [λtDA pDA_t + ρt↑ r_t↑ + ρt↓ r_t↓ − λt,ωimb z_tω − cDG g_tω] Δt − Cωage.

The retail load-service overlay used for reporting is not included in the optimization and is added after the wholesale solve. This prevents the overlay from changing the ranking of the market decisions.

The risk objective is:

(29) max (1−β) Σω πω Πω + β CVaRα(Π).

Because the implementation maximizes profit, the lower-tail CVaR is represented through the Rockafellar–Uryasev auxiliary formulation. Define loss Lω = −Πω. Then:

(30) CVaRα(L) = ζ + 1/(1−α) Σω πω sω.

(31) sω ≥ Lω − ζ, sω ≥ 0, ∀ω.

Substitution of Lω = −Πω into (30)–(31) yields a linear optimization model. The reported β = 0.05 and α = 0.95 therefore introduce tail sensitivity without requiring a nonlinear risk constraint.

L. Complete LP Statement

The implemented problem can be summarized as:

(32) max (1−β) Σω πω Πω − β[ζ + 1/(1−α) Σω πω sω]

subject to (4)–(24) and the selected aging-law equations (21), (24), or (25)–(27), together with (30)–(31), variable bounds, and the common market parameters. The first-stage variables pDA, r↑, and r↓ are scenario-independent; recourse variables are scenario-dependent. Every term in (32) is affine in the decision variables. Consequently, the final optimization is an LP.

No Big-M, McCormick envelope, SOS constraint, or KKT reformulation is required in the implemented model. Piecewise linearization is required only for the Xu depth cost and the band representation of ReLCCA; CVaR is linearized with auxiliary variables. This explicit accounting of linearization is important because it distinguishes the present model from nonlinear bi-level formulations that require KKT or strong-duality transformations.

V. EXPERIMENTAL DESIGN AND DIAGNOSTIC PROTOCOL

The evaluation uses four steps. Step 1 establishes ordinary-day agreement by solving all three aging laws on winter, spring, and summer isolation days and on two rolling weeks. Step 2 freezes the 19 August residual and replaces hours 9–16 with scaled copies of the 1 May trough, with the minimum varied from −50 to −499 EUR/MWh. Step 3 decomposes wholesale profit on any day where the model ranking changes. Step 4 scans every day in the study panel whose minimum day-ahead price is below −20 EUR/MWh and performs the specified robustness checks.

The experimental design is intentionally paired: the plant, scenario construction, solver, reserve schedule, and market-price inputs are held constant while the aging law is changed. This is necessary for attributing a difference in realized profit to the aging representation rather than to a different market or uncertainty model.

VII. DISCUSSION

The results support a precise interpretation of calendar-aware degradation. On ordinary residuals, the additional occupancy cost does not necessarily change the qualitative value of reserve participation. Under the 1 May residual, however, the throughput energy-only solution becomes long during a severe negative-price interval. ReLCCA assigns an additional cost to maintaining high-energy occupancy and reserve parking, changing the optimal first-stage day-ahead position.

The decomposition is particularly important. It would be misleading to state that the bid reversal occurs because ReLCCA simply produces a much larger degradation bill. Table VI shows the opposite: the direct aging-cost difference between the two energy-only cases is only a few euros, while the day-ahead energy-position difference is 1,246 EUR. The aging representation acts as a shaping term inside the optimization and changes the market position; the downstream financial effect is therefore much larger than the direct aging charge.

The controlled injection reinforces this interpretation. A negative trough of −499 EUR/MWh imposed on an otherwise ordinary summer residual does not produce the same reversal. The residual portfolio state determines whether the energy-only model would exploit the trough by moving long. Hence the mechanism cannot be summarized as 'negative prices cause ReLCCA to outperform.' The defensible statement is that explicit occupancy pricing can alter the bid when the residual conditions make high-SoC positioning economically attractive under a sufficiently adverse price path.

The result also clarifies the role of reserve. The reserve product is not modeled as a detailed activation-energy settlement. Instead, upward reserve is coupled to parked energy through (15), and the published block-average capacity prices provide the capacity-revenue side. This is sufficient for the diagnostic question—whether reserve parking changes the economics of occupancy—but not sufficient for claiming day-specific aFRR settlement accuracy.

VIII. LIMITATIONS AND REPRODUCIBILITY

Several limitations bound the claims. First, the diagnosed event occurs once in 262 complete days, and September–November are absent from the study panel. The frequency of such events therefore cannot be estimated reliably from this sample. Second, ReLCCA is a linear market encoding of published NMC calendar/cycle-aging relationships rather than a cell-level electrochemical model. The three-plateau κ map is an operational parameterization, not an independently validated laboratory fit. Third, the linear annualization used in the baseline does not reproduce the exact square-root time dependence of calendar aging; the reported √t sensitivity is therefore a robustness test rather than a replacement electrochemical model.

Fourth, aFRR capacity prices are repeated four-hour block means rather than day-specific cleared auction records. Fifth, imbalance prices are a documented two-price proxy rather than directly observed reBAP settlements. Sixth, the model does not simulate the detailed activation-energy trajectory of aFRR. Seventh, the Xu benchmark is a daily-depth piecewise-linear model and is not equivalent to a rainflow implementation. Finally, the study uses a price-taking market role and does not model strategic price-making or network constraints.

The computational package uses Python/Pyomo with HiGHS. All reported optimization programs use the stated seed and common input data. The study should therefore be reproduced from the preserved project data and configuration rather than from manually transcribed numerical tables.

IX. CONCLUSION

This paper presented a controlled diagnostic for identifying when explicit calendar-aging exposure can change a risk-aware BESS energy–reserve bid. Three aging laws were embedded in one linear mean–CVaR market formulation, with ReLCCA extending cycle-aware operation by pricing SoC occupancy and reserve parking. On ordinary days, the three laws largely agree in the qualitative value of reserve participation. On 1 May 2026, however, the throughput energy-only solution becomes long in a −499 EUR/MWh midday trough while ReLCCA remains short. The wholesale decomposition shows that the associated 1,312 EUR difference between energy-only books is dominated by the day-ahead position rather than by the direct aging bill.

The controlled trough experiment and 20-day negative-price scan show why the result should be interpreted as a mechanism diagnosis rather than a universal threshold. Negative prices alone do not reproduce the reversal; the residual portfolio state must also make the energy-only position sufficiently exposed to the trough. The principal methodological contribution is therefore the diagnostic separation of physical aging cost from the market-position response induced by the aging model. Future work should replace reserve and imbalance proxies with day-specific German settlement data, extend the sample to a complete calendar year, and validate the occupancy-aging coefficients against measured cell or BESS field data.
