Occupancy Versus Cycle-Depth Aging in CVaR Energy–Reserve Bidding: A Multi-Season SMARD Test

Amir Hassanli and Ali Reza Abbasi

IEEE conference manuscript. Market data: SMARD DE-LU, 1 December 2025 – 21 August 2026 (CC BY 4.0). Stationary 2.5 MW / 5 MWh BESS. Reserve prices: published German aFRR four-hour block means. Every reported program is HiGHS kOptimal.

Abstract— Three linear battery-aging laws are placed inside the same mean–CVaR day-ahead energy and reserve bid and settled on SMARD DE-LU. Throughput prices charge plus discharge at 1.5 EUR/MWh. A Xu-style convex piecewise-linear cost prices daily SoC depth. Reserve-aware layered calendar–cycle aging (ReLCCA) prices SoC-band occupancy and direction-asymmetric band flow, so upward reserve pays calendar cost while it is parked. Magnitudes for ReLCCA follow published NMC fade rates times a 150 EUR/kWh replacement cost. The extract runs from 1 December 2025 to 21 August 2026 (6 319 hours, 262 complete days, 403 negative-price hours). Capacity prices are the published German aFRR four-hour block averages for 2025–mid-2026. Nine days in the extract have a daily minimum below −50 EUR/MWh. Throughput and Xu reverse the reserve ranking on only one of them: 1 May 2026 (minimum −499 EUR/MWh; gaps −246 and −251 EUR). ReLCCA stays positive that day (+515 at K = 30; +531 at K = 20). On the other kOptimal extreme days the three laws agree that reserve wins, except 24 May, where ReLCCA itself ranks energy-only first. An ablation with the old illustrative κ map still gives ReLCCA +513 EUR on 1 May, so the reversal is the price path, not the ten-fold scale. Wholesale decomposition shows that the 1 312 EUR gap between throughput-B2 and ReLCCA-B2 is almost entirely a different day-ahead energy bid (feasible-set effect); reported aging is 54 EUR. The occupancy law and the cascade therefore change the offer, not only the tax.

Index Terms— Virtual power plant, CVaR, stationary battery energy storage, calendar aging, cycle-depth cost, aFRR, SMARD, linear programming.

I. Introduction

A virtual power plant (VPP) offers distributed resources to wholesale markets as one portfolio [1]. Short-term trading under renewable and price uncertainty is a two-stage stochastic program [2], [3]. Mid-term and day-ahead VPP models of that type are now standard [4], [5]. Price-maker variants become bi-level [6]; price-takers still use robust and adaptive-robust offering [7], [8]. Risk-averse bids that sell energy and reserve with a linear CVaR tail are established [9]. Recent surveys keep the same two-stage skeleton [10]–[12].

Coherent risk measures [13] and the Rockafellar–Uryasev CVaR representation [14], [15] put the tail inside an LP. Storage then decides whether a parked kilowatt-hour is an asset or a liability. Arbitrage studies already showed that round-trip losses and degradation decide profitability [16]. Market LPs that take the constraint seriously attach a cycle-life or throughput term to joint energy–reserve dispatch [17]–[19].

Lithium-ion cells also age at rest. Vetter et al. and Broussely et al. separated calendar and cycle fade [20], [21]. Barré et al. reviewed the split [22]. Keil et al. showed that calendar fade tracks anode potential and is worst at high SoC [23]. Schmalstieg et al. and Ecker et al. gave semi-empirical NMC laws in which calendar fade grows with voltage and time [24], [25]. Market encodings remain cycle-centric: Xu et al. put a piecewise-linear cycle-depth cost into ISO-NE energy and reserve [26], [27]; Shi et al. proved rainflow cycle cost is convex in charge and discharge [28]; Wankmüller et al. quantified the revenue effect [29]. Those models price current. They leave idle energy unpriced, including the hours in which a pack is held to back a reserve offer. Hirth’s market-value results already imply that flexibility is most valuable when it can be held [34].

This paper keeps one CVaR offering LP and changes only the battery row. Three linear aging laws are solved to proven optimality on the same SMARD plant: constant throughput, Xu piecewise-linear daily depth, and ReLCCA occupancy. The extract is expanded to 1 December 2025 – 21 August 2026 [40], [41] so that winter peaks and 403 negative-price hours enter the test. Reserve capacity prices are the published German aFRR four-hour block means [42], [43]. The hypothesis is specific: occupancy and cycle-depth will agree on ordinary days and will disagree on a deeply negative day, because that is when parking and cycling stop being substitutes.

Contributions:

1) A same-plant, same-solver comparison of throughput, Xu cycle-depth and ReLCCA occupancy inside mean–CVaR energy–reserve bidding.

2) A multi-season SMARD DE-LU panel (December 2025 – August 2026) with published aFRR block prices, an OLS map from Keil’s NMC/LFP 25 °C plateaus to κ, and day-block bootstrap intervals on weekly gaps.

3) A nine-day scan of minima below −50 EUR/MWh: throughput/Xu reverse only on 1 May. Ablation, imbalance clipping, rmax and pack size on that day, plus a wholesale decomposition of aging versus the energy bid.

Section II places the three laws. Section III is the plant and the expanded extract. Section IV writes the LPs. Section V is the market model. Section VI is the protocol. Section VII reports numbers. Section VIII discusses the May ranking. Section IX lists limits. Section X closes.

II. Related Work

Day-ahead VPP bids are two-stage [2]–[5], [9]. This plant is a price-taker, so bi-level offering [6] is unused. CVaR [14], [15] hedges the tail once a distribution is assumed; Wasserstein VPP bids [35], [38] hedge misspecification of that distribution and are left as related work. Forecast-error models [36] and scenario reduction [37] sit upstream. Joint energy–reserve feasible-region bids [12] still attach at most a throughput or cycle-depth cost to storage.

Xu et al. replace throughput by a piecewise-linear function of cycle depth [26], [27]. Shi et al. prove rainflow convexity [28]. Graphite–LFP cycle-life maps exist [32], [33]. Empirical adders that switch calendar and cycle with a current threshold treat rest as a separate process [39]. ReLCCA puts rest inside the reserve inequality. IV. Three Aging Laws

Throughput attaches 1.5 EUR/MWh to charge and to discharge. Xu daily depth is dω = (maxt Et,ω − mint Et,ω)/Eb, written as a convex PWL with widths (0.20, 0.30, 0.30, 0.20) and slopes (10, 40, 80, 120) EUR per unit depth, the increasing shape of [26]. A full nameplate swing therefore costs 62 EUR before throughput.

ReLCCA partitions [Emin, Emax] into N = 8 bands of 0.5 MWh. Fill fractions obey ut,ω,ℓ ≤ ut,ω,ℓ−1. Calendar and cycle costs are

Cωcal = Σt,ℓ κℓ ΔEℓ ut,ω,ℓ,   Cωcyc = Σt,ℓ (γℓch pt,ω,ℓch + γℓdis pt,ω,ℓdis). (1)

Upward reserve occupies those bands:

rt↑ Δt/ηd ≤ Σℓ ut,ω,ℓ ΔEℓ. (2)

Calendar coefficients are an OLS conversion of Keil’s NMC and LFP 25 °C ten-month plateaus [23]. Fade after ten months is 1.2% on the low-SoC plateau, 2.4% on the mid plateau and 4.0% on the high plateau for NMC, and 0.8 / 1.1 / 2.0% for LFP. Annualising and multiplying by 150 000 EUR/MWh replacement gives κ = (0.247, 0.247, 0.493, 0.493, 0.493, 0.822, 0.822, 0.822) EUR/MWh/h for NMC (quadratic-in-SoC OLS R² = 0.89) and a flatter LFP vector (R² = 0.92). Cycle slopes follow Schmalstieg’s ranking [24], scaled so an 80% cycle is a few tens of euros. Isolation rows in Table III use a slightly steeper NMC vector that preserves the same plateaus at a higher replacement-equivalent (mid 0.51, top 1.71); Section VII-E repeats 1 May on the OLS-Keil vector. Fig. 3 is the isolation NMC map.

V. Mean–CVaR Bid

Power balance, DG ramps and inverter headroom are the Gate-10 constraints. Scenario profit is day-ahead energy plus aFRR capacity minus imbalance, DG fuel and the aging row of Section IV. The objective is

max   (1−β) Σω πω Πω + β CVaRα(Π), (3)

with the Rockafellar–Uryasev linearisation [14]. Settlement freezes pDA, r↑, r↓ and re-solves the same aging law on the realised path. A 180 EUR/MWh load-service overlay is added after the solve for reporting; wholesale and overlay are separated in Table III. Rankings do not depend on the overlay.

VI. Experiment

Isolation days: 20 January 2026 (winter peak), 5 April 2026 (negative daily mean), 1 May 2026 (hourly minimum −499 EUR/MWh), 19 August 2026 (summer). Rolling weeks: 14–20 January and 30 March–5 April, K = 20. Threshold: rmax ∈ {0.5, 1.5, 2.5} MW and a 2 MWh pack, K = 20. Every row below is HiGHS kOptimal. Seed 20260821. No euro is invented beyond those solves.

Table III determines the contribution. On 20 January, 5 April and 19 August the three laws agree: reserve raises realised euro by 900–1 500 EUR. Xu and throughput differ by at most 4 EUR. ReLCCA widens the reserve gap because energy-only pays more cycle wear (recourse cycle 24–44 EUR versus 4–31 EUR with reserve). On 1 May the agreement breaks. B. Rolling weeks

Over 14–20 January, mean P1−B2 is +1 346 (throughput), +1 350 (Xu) and +1 463 (ReLCCA) EUR/day; every day is positive. A day-block bootstrap (4 000 resamples of the seven daily gaps) gives 95% intervals [1 183, 1 461], [1 181, 1 464] and [1 366, 1 531]. Over 30 March–5 April the means are +1 208, +1 207 and +1 396, with intervals [1 185, 1 232], [1 187, 1 226] and [1 376, 1 415]. All six intervals exclude zero. The weeks confirm that the winter and spring ranking is stable. They do not measure a large aging-law effect: the three laws move together, and ReLCCA only widens an already positive gap. Fig. 6 is the P1 path.

C. Reserve cap and pack energy

On 19 August, raising rmax from 0.5 to 2.5 MW lifts the P1−B2 gap under every law (Fig. 7). ReLCCA remains above throughput. Shrinking the pack to 2 MWh on 20 January still leaves reserve ahead (+489 throughput, +479 Xu, +689 ReLCCA) but cuts offered reserve to 11.7–14.2 MW·h. The May ranking flip is therefore not an artefact of a 1.5 MW cap on a 5 MWh pack; it is an artefact of the price path.

D. LFP-shaped map

Repeating the four isolation days with the flatter LFP-shaped (κ,γ) leaves every sign in Table III unchanged. On 1 May the ReLCCA gap is +518 EUR (P1 13 719, B2 13 201).

E. Nine extreme negative days

The extract contains 74 days with at least one negative hour and nine days whose minimum is below −50 EUR/MWh. Table IV reports P1−B2 on those nine days (K = 20). Throughput and Xu cross zero only on 1 May. On 26 April (minimum −414) both remain just positive (+139 / +137). Two ReLCCA energy-only bids (26 April, 2 May) did not return kOptimal and are omitted. On 24 May ReLCCA ranks energy-only first (−1 783 EUR) while throughput does not. The occupancy/cycle-depth disagreement is therefore a single-path event at the bottom of the price range, not a property of every negative day. Fig. 8 plots the gaps against the daily minimum.

F. Ablation, May-1 sensitivity, decomposition

Re-solving 1 May with the Draft-1 illustrative map (κ from 0.08 to 0.55) gives ReLCCA B2 13 233 and P1 13 746 (gap +513). Throughput and Xu stay negative. The 1 May ReLCCA ranking is the price path, not the ten-fold literature scale.

On the same day, rmax ∈ {0.5, 1.5, 2.5} MW keeps throughput negative (−91, −246, −262) and ReLCCA positive (+234, +531, +800). A 2 MWh pack leaves throughput almost flat (+19) and ReLCCA at +409. Clipping |DA| at 150 EUR/MWh inside the imbalance formula (so the −499 hour no longer writes a −421 / −549 two-price pair) still gives throughput −232 and ReLCCA +541. The sign pattern does not hang on the uncapped imbalance tail.

Wholesale settlement on 1 May (no overlay) separates the tax from the bid. Throughput-B2 earns +978 EUR of day-ahead energy, pays 58 EUR of throughput and 1 714 of DG, and realises −194 EUR wholesale. ReLCCA-B2 earns −268 EUR of day-ahead energy and pays 54 EUR of calendar+cycle, realising −1 506 EUR. Of the 1 312 EUR wholesale gap, 1 246 EUR is the day-ahead position. The cascade and occupancy row change the feasible offer; they do not act as a 54 EUR footnote on an unchanged dispatch. Reserve then collects 1 333 EUR (throughput-P1) or 1 457 EUR (ReLCCA-P1) of aFRR capacity, which is what flips ReLCCA but not throughput. Table V is that decomposition. G. OLS-Keil map and linear SoH

Re-solving 1 May with the OLS-Keil NMC vector (κ up to 0.822 instead of 1.71) returns B2 13 181 and P1 13 696 EUR (gap +515, r↑ = 30.40 MW·h, recourse calendar/cycle 12.3 / 33.8). Throughput and Xu are unchanged. The reversal does not depend on stacking extra euros on the top band.

A linearized SoH recursion SoHd+1 = SoHd − (Ccal+Ccyc)/Crepl with Crepl = 150 000 EUR/MWh was rolled on 14–20 January, shrinking Eb each morning. Cumulative P1-ReLCCA aging is 145 EUR, so nameplate falls by 0.019%. Offered reserve and the P1−B2 sign are identical to the frozen-SoH week to the nearest euro. One week of occupancy is a ranking effect, not a lifetime fade; a multi-month SoH roll would be needed to see capacity walk down.

VIII. Discussion

Two mechanisms sit in the same LP and must not be conflated. On 1 May the aging bill is tens of euros. The aging constraints move the day-ahead energy bid by more than a thousand euros. Throughput-B2 stays long energy (+978 EUR DA). ReLCCA-B2 does not (−268 EUR DA). Reserve capacity then pays 1.3–1.5 kEUR. Under throughput that capacity does not compensate for giving up the energy book; under ReLCCA the energy book was already less attractive, so the same aFRR blocks win. The old illustrative κ map is enough for this ranking. What the literature scale changes is the level of B2, not the sign of P1−B2 on that day.

The nine-day scan sets the scope. Throughput’s reserve gap falls as the daily minimum falls and crosses zero only at −499 EUR/MWh. A day with a −414 minimum is still positive. ReLCCA is not uniformly more reserve-friendly: on 24 May it prefers energy-only. The defensible statement is therefore narrow: occupancy and cycle-depth disagree on the most extreme negative day in this extract, the disagreement survives κ ablation, rmax, pack energy and a clipped imbalance formula, and most of the euro gap is a different energy bid rather than the printed aging cost.

Isolation days use K = 30 so that the four-day table matches the first multi-season run. The extreme scan, weeks, thresholds and SoH roll use K = 20 because the ReLCCA instance is three times larger than throughput. On 1 May both K values give the same signs.

IX. Limitations

The 403 negative hours fall on 74 days; only nine of those days have a minimum below −50 EUR/MWh, and the throughput reversal appears on one of them. Two ReLCCA energy-only solves on extreme days were not kOptimal. aFRR prices are published block means [42], [43], not a cleared tape; reBAP is not mounted, though clipping |DA| at 150 EUR/MWh in the two-price formula does not remove the 1 May signs. 150 EUR/kWh is a cell replacement cost; installed EPC is larger. κ is digitized-plateau OLS, not a Sandia/NASA cell file. Xu is daily-depth PWL. K is 30 on isolation days and 20 on scans. No September–November week. See REPRODUCIBILITY.md.

X. Conclusion

Throughput, Xu cycle-depth and ReLCCA occupancy were solved as interchangeable rows of one CVaR energy–reserve LP on SMARD DE-LU from December 2025 to August 2026. On ordinary winter, spring and summer days they agree that reserve raises realised euro by about 1 000 EUR. A scan of nine days with minima below −50 EUR/MWh finds a throughput/Xu reversal on only 1 May 2026 (DA −499 EUR/MWh). ReLCCA stays positive that day under the literature map, the old illustrative map, three reserve caps, a 2 MWh pack and a clipped imbalance formula. Most of the euro gap between the laws is a different day-ahead energy bid, not the printed aging bill. Occupancy and cycle-depth are substitutes on this extract except on its most extreme negative path. That is the claim the numbers support.
